The named concepts of not getting caught

You already do most of this by instinct. The point of this page is to hand you the vocabulary — the "defensive-programming"-style umbrella terms — so you can reason about your scraper in named patterns instead of vibes. Everything sorts into one question: avoidance or resilience.

Two questions, three camps

Every anti-detection idea answers one of two questions: "how do I not get detected?" (detection-avoidance) or "how do I survive being blocked or failing without making it worse?" (detection-resilience). Your situation forces a third, practical camp: "if I do get caught, how small is the damage?" — risk-containment. That three-way split is the spine of everything below.

The one strategic fact that makes the vocabulary worth learning: you face defense in depth — Akamai (network/TLS) → fingerprint → Arkose (behavioral) → challenge, stacked. You can't "win" one layer. A perfect real-browser fingerprint is wasted if your timing is robotic, because the next layer up catches it.

Camp 1 — Evasion (don't get detected)

Your strongest design instinct, named: you lean away from spoofing and toward being a real-enough client. That's fingerprint coherence — a real headed Chrome is automatically self-consistent (its UA, WebGL, TLS, fonts all agree), whereas spoofing risks contradictions that detectors catch. It's why running real Chrome and not faking the mouse is correct, not lazy.

You avoid the riskiest action through session reuse (a warm persistent profile that skips login) gated by a liveness probe (the cheap "am I still logged in?" check). The gap is at the keyboard: humanization — typing char-by-char with varied cadence — because the defender layer you're exposed to is behavioral biometrics (scoring keystroke and mouse timing). Pasting a password in one event is the textbook tell it catches.

And the subtle one: the fix for robotic timing is not "add randomness." It's distribution matching — sampling from the shape humans actually produce (log-normal, heavy-tailed), so your timing is statistically indistinguishable from human. Naïve uniform randomness backfires; that failure mode has a name — the jitter trap — and it's the demo below. (Your fixed 3-second waits are a crude politeness policy; the upgrade is making the spacing log-normal, not constant.)

Camp 2 — Resilience (survive failures & blocks)

The single highest-risk thing your scraper does is log in, and the signal that freezes accounts is login-velocity detection — too many logins per unit time. Back-to-back re-auth is exactly this. The countermeasures are all named patterns: pace retries with exponential backoff; hard-cap the riskiest operation with a circuit breaker (stop entirely after N, cool down); bound the whole loop with a deadline (wall-clock budget).

You already nail two resilience patterns. Resuming from the next un-scraped window instead of redoing work is durable execution (checkpoint-and-resume), and it only stays safe because re-scraping a window doesn't corrupt data — idempotency. And detecting an Arkose challenge and bailing rather than submitting a junk token is fail-safe degradation (fail closed) — correct, because a fake/empty solve is a high-confidence bot tell.

Camp 3 — Risk-containment (limit the damage)

Your one personal account is your entire blast radius, so everything risk-related is least privilege / blast-radius containment: capping and pacing logins are blast-radius controls on your highest-privilege identity. And the reason "don't rotate the profile" is a rule, not a preference, is reputation-based detection — detectors score an identity by its history; a stable, long-lived, clean profile accrues trust, and rotating throws it away (and looks like evasion). Minimizing logins, bailing on Arkose, and keeping one profile all point the same direction: protect accrued trust.

The jitter trap

This is the most counterintuitive idea, so make it clickable. The bars are a histogram of the delays your scraper waits between actions. Drag human from 0 (pure random.uniform()) toward 100 (log-normal, human-shaped) and watch the flat slab collapse into a right-skewed hump with a long tail. The lesson: adding uniform randomness doesn't hide you — a flat distribution is a shape no human produces, so it's its own fingerprint.

Part II — what matters most

Ranked for your case

Not all named concepts weigh the same for an authenticated airline scraper. In order:

1 · Login-velocity → circuit breaker + backoff

Your back-to-back 4× re-auth is the single biggest account-freeze trigger. Cap it low (circuit breaker) and space it (backoff). Highest leverage, by far.

2 · Reputation-based detection

The unifying "why" behind don't-rotate, bail-on-Arkose, and minimize-logins: protect the accrued trust on one identity.

3 · Behavioral biometrics → distribution matching

Your real fingerprint is wasted while your timing is robotic. Defeat behavioral biometrics with log-normal distribution matching — and stop walking into the jitter trap.

4 · Blast-radius containment

One personal account = your whole risk surface. Every login you avoid shrinks the blast radius.

5 · Durable execution + deadline

You already checkpoint per window (durable execution); adding the deadline completes the loop.

6 · Already right — keep them

Fingerprint coherence and fail-safe degradation: the two instincts you got right. Now you have their names, so you'll keep them on purpose.

Glossary
Avoidance vs. resilience
The organizing axis: avoiding detection vs. surviving failure/blocks. Plus a third camp — risk-containment — for limiting damage if caught.
Defense in depth
Detection stacked in independent layers (network → fingerprint → behavioral → challenge); beating one still gets caught by the next.
Fingerprint coherence
All client signals must be mutually consistent. A real browser is coherent for free; spoofing risks contradictions detectors flag.
Session reuse
Reuse a persistent authenticated profile so you appear as an ongoing user and skip the high-risk login flow.
Liveness probe
A cheap pre-flight that confirms the session is still authenticated before deciding to re-log-in.
Humanization
Making automated input look human — char-by-char typing with realistic, variable cadence rather than one-shot fills.
Behavioral biometrics
The defender technique that scores keystroke/mouse/scroll timing to separate humans from bots, after fingerprint checks pass.
Distribution matching
Don't just randomize — sample from the human distribution (log-normal, heavy-tailed) so timing is statistically indistinguishable.
The jitter trap
When your randomness is itself a fingerprint: uniform random.uniform() makes a flat distribution no human produces.
Crawler politeness policy
Self-imposed spacing of requests/actions; the crude version is a fixed delay, the good version is log-normal spacing.
Login-velocity detection
The #1 account-takeover signal: too many logins per unit time from one identity/device. Back-to-back re-auth trips it.
Exponential backoff
On repeated failure, wait progressively longer between retries (with jitter to de-synchronize).
Circuit breaker
After N failures, stop trying entirely for a cooldown instead of hammering a hostile endpoint. A hard re-auth cap is one.
Deadline / time budget
A hard wall-clock upper bound on how long a loop may run before it gives up cleanly.
Durable execution
Persist progress so an interrupted long job resumes from the last completed step instead of redoing work (checkpoint-and-resume).
Idempotency
A retried step doesn't double-apply; re-scraping a window can't corrupt data. The required companion to durable execution.
Fail-safe degradation
When you can't proceed safely, degrade to a safe non-action (fail closed) — e.g. bail on a challenge instead of guessing.
Least privilege / blast radius
Limit the scope of damage one compromise can cause. Your single account is your entire blast radius.
Reputation-based detection
Scoring an identity/device by its history; trust accrues to stable, long-lived clean identities and is destroyed by suspicious events.
